How these numbers are calculated

Training & embodied footprint — the bigger picture

A single query is only the inference (running) part — running the model to answer you. "Embodied" means the footprint of building it: manufacturing the chips and servers it runs on, plus training the model itself. That one-time build cost is real but is spread across billions of queries, so running (inference) usually still adds up to more overall.

Linear scale shows absolute bar lengths; smaller values may be difficult to see beside training emissions. Exact values are shown on each bar.

Ecosia: search and optional AI

For common lookups, Ecosia combines regular web search with optional AI Overviews and AI Chat. You can switch all AI features off in Settings, then use AI only when it is genuinely helpful.

Ecosia says it uses smaller, more efficient models, avoids video generation and produces more renewable electricity than its AI uses. These are company-reported claims, not independent per-query measurements.

Why these tips

Peer-reviewed measurements link longer outputs and response times with higher inference energy. The IEA also finds that reasoning, image, video and agentic tasks can use hundreds or thousands of times more energy than simple text.

AI infrastructure outlook

Global data-centre electricity demand

Demand is projected to rise from about 415 TWh in 2024 to 945 TWh in 2030—more than doubling in six years. The intervening years illustrate the trajectory; the endpoints carry the evidence.

Australian electricity and water demand

Data-centre electricity

Estimated demand rises from about 4 TWh today to 21.4 TWh by 2035.

Industry water use

Projected demand rises from 5.5 GL to about 17 GL by 2030; local effects depend on siting and cooling.

Australia: operating capacity versus proposals

Operating capacity is forecast to grow from 1.4 GW to about 3.2 GW by 2030. Connection queues and announced pipelines show interest—not committed capacity—and should not be added together.
